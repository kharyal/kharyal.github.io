## Abstract

Learning reward functions from human feedback is a central challenge in
reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Most existing approaches
cast preference learning as a classification problem — predicting which of two
trajectories a human prefers — and optimize a cross-entropy loss derived from
the Bradley-Terry model. While effective, this framing discards the *degree*
of preference and can be brittle when feedback is noisy or transitivity
violations are present.

We propose **Ranking Mean Squared Error (RMSE)**, a novel reward learning
objective that treats human feedback as a regression signal over pairwise
rankings. By directly regressing the reward margin implied by a preference
pair, RMSE encourages the learned reward function to produce scores that
are *metrically consistent* with the expressed preference strengths, not
merely ordinally correct. We show theoretically that RMSE minimization
provides a tighter bound on ranking regret than cross-entropy under
mild assumptions on the feedback distribution, and empirically that it
yields better reward models across a range of preference learning benchmarks.
